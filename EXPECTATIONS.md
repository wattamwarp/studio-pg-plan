# Studio P.G. Building — Design Expectations

Living brief. Everything the design must satisfy, in the client's own terms, with the
architect's reading of each point. Updated 27 Aug 2026.

## 1. The plot

| Boundary | Length | Character | Setback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Front** (road, south) | 38.8 ft | Street frontage | **0 — build to the line** |
| **Left** (garden, west) | 50.2 ft | **Slanted.** Open garden beyond | 1′-0″ |
| **Rear** (north) | 28.8 ft | **Blind** — no ventilation | 1′-0″ |
| **Right** (Sikandar sir, east) | 47.7 ft | **Essentially straight. Blind** — no ventilation | 1′-0″ |

Derived (right angle assumed at the front-right corner, since the right boundary is the
straight one):

- Plot area **1,640.2 sq ft** (182.2 sq yd · 152.4 sq m)
- Buildable after setbacks **1,515.4 sq ft**
- Usable width **36.8 ft at the front, 27.2 ft at the rear** — the plot loses ~9.6 ft of
  width, all of it off the **west** edge
- East wall runs dead straight for 46′-9″

> **Open question — blocking.** Four side lengths do not fix a quadrilateral. One diagonal
> (front-left to rear-right) would remove the assumption. Until then every area here carries
> roughly ±2 % uncertainty.

## 2. The governing constraint: only two sides can breathe

This is the decision that drives the whole plan.

- **Garden (west) and road (south) are the only ventilated edges.** ~89 ft of usable
  frontage between them.
- **Sikandar (east) and rear (north) are blind.** No habitable room may rely on them.
- Therefore: **push rooms onto the garden side**, accept that they follow the slant, and put
  circulation, the core and anything windowless into the blind north-east zone.
- Rooms may be **slightly slanted** to follow the garden boundary — explicitly accepted by
  the client.

Any blind pocket too deep to reach daylight must become an **open-to-sky light court**, or
be left unbuilt. It must not become a dark room.

## 3. Accommodation

| Item | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Room count | **Minimum 8** per typical floor. More is better if they stay comfortable. |
| Room type | Studio — sleeping, storage, attached washroom, balcony |
| Occupancy | **2 beds per room** |
| Module size | **≤ 150–160 sq ft built-up** (whole unit incl. its share of walls) |
| Washroom | **Extended** — real toilet + bath, not a shower cubicle |
| Balcony | **Short in length**, compact — not a long thin ledge |
| Excluded | No lobby, no shaft, no laundry, no store, no other extra components |

## 4. Furniture and clearances

| Item | Size | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bed × 2 | 6′-0″ × 2′-6″ | **Split, not pushed together** — a gap between them |
| Wardrobe | 4′-0″ × 1′-11″ | On a free wall, door swing must clear |
| Entry | — | **On opening the room door there must be clear floor to walk into.** No bed in the entry path. |

The entry clearance rule is a hard one. The door swing plus a walking strip must land on
empty floor, and the route from the door to the balcony and to the washroom must not cross a
bed.

## 6. Objectives, in priority order

1. **Every room ventilates** — garden, road or an open court. No dark rooms.
2. **At least 8 rooms** per floor.
3. **Comfortable rooms** — split beds, clear entry, usable washroom.
4. **Maximum floor area** — stretch the envelope wherever it is legal and sensible.
5. Modules at or under the 150–160 sq ft ceiling.

Where 2 and 3 conflict, show the trade explicitly as separate variations rather than
splitting the difference silently.

## 7. Known tensions to resolve

| Tension | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Room count vs module size | A 150–160 sq ft module needs ~13 × 12 ft. Eight of those plus corridor and core exceed the floor. Something gives. |
| Ventilation vs coverage | Only two edges breathe, so the blind north-east cannot be filled with rooms. A light court costs area but buys habitability. |
| Corridor length | A deep, narrow plot forces a long corridor. At 3′-0″ it still eats ~11 % of the floor — and corridors, unlike stairs and lifts, **are counted in FAR**. |
| Single stair | One 3′-0″ stair may not satisfy escape rules above three storeys over a 46 ft corridor. |
| **The 20-bed cliff** | Above 20 sleeping occupants the building reclassifies from NBC A-2 to **A-3 Dormitory**, forcing a 4′-1″ stair, 300 mm tread, 150 mm riser and 1.25 m corridor. 8 rooms × 4 floors = 64 beds is firmly A-3, so **the 3′-0″ stair and 3′-0″ corridor asked for are not compliant at that scale.** |

## 7a. Rules confirmed by research (see `RESEARCH-FINDINGS.md`)

These are now fixed inputs, not open questions.

| Rule | Value | Effect on the plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bathrooms may be internal** | MBBL 4.25.4 permits mechanical ventilation for toilets, **not** for habitable rooms | Push every bathroom to the blind east; give all 86 ft of good frontage to bedrooms |
| Bathroom vent shaft, ≤12 m | 2.8 m², min side 1.2 m | Or mechanical extract per stack |
| **Daylit room depth** | **11′-10″** from the window (BRE); 7.5 m legal maximum | Bedroom depth is capped. Bathroom goes behind it, in the dim zone. |
| Interior court for a habitable room | **3.0 m × 3.0 m** minimum | The only way to make the blind corner habitable |
| Stair + lift | **Free of FAR** | Never shave them |
| Corridor | **Counted in FAR** | The only servant space worth minimising |
| Cantilevered wardrobe niche | 0.60–0.75 m, **exempt from covered area** | Push the 4′-0″ wardrobe out of the room, ~8 sq ft/room recovered |
| Balcony cantilever | Free of FAR — but **blocked here**, a 1′-0″ setback leaves nowhere to project | Balconies must stay inside the building line |
| Daylit floor area available | **1,086 of 1,515 sq ft (71.7 %)** | 7 rooms at 155 sq ft, or 8 at 136 sq ft. Nothing in between. |

### Ergonomic minimums now binding

| Clearance | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Gap between the two beds | **2′-0″ absolute, 2′-6″ target** |
| Foot-of-bed route to bath/balcony | **3′-0″** |
| Clear floor inside the room door | **3′-0″ × 6′-0″**, entirely outside the door swing |
| Bathroom | 5′-0″ × 7′-0″ for a real wet/dry split |
| Wardrobe and bathroom doors | **Sliding** — never inward-swinging |

> **Recommendation on bed size.** 2′-6″ is narrower than every published single-bed standard
> (UK/EU, Neufert and the Indian market are all 3′-0″). Going to 3′-0″ costs 12″ of room width
> across both beds and materially improves lettability.

## 8. Statutory reality check — not yet verified

Flagged so they are not forgotten. All need confirmation against the local byelaw.

- Zero front setback is rarely permitted; the front margin usually carries services.
- Ground coverage in the 80 %+ range exceeds most residential limits.
- Paying-guest / hostel use can trigger parking, fire and lift-lobby requirements.
- Habitable rooms normally need an openable area of ~1/10 of floor area onto open space —
  which is exactly why the blind east cannot hold rooms.
- With no setback anywhere useful, there is **no on-site parking**. If parking is required
  the ground floor becomes a stilt and the typical floor starts one level up.

### Two risks that could invalidate the scheme outright

**Hostel land-use minimum plot size.** MBBL 3.5.2 sets Guest House / Boarding House / Hostel
land use at **minimum plot 500 m² on a 20 m road**. Madhya Pradesh Reg. 4.3.8 requires >500 m²
on a 12 m road in large cities. **This plot is 152 m².** If the authority processes the
building as a *hostel land use* rather than as a residential building let room-wise, the plot
is disqualified on size alone. **Settle this before any sanction drawing is started.**

**Ground coverage.** The current footprint is ~82 % of the plot. Delhi caps 100–250 m² plots at
**75 %**, BBMP at **65 %**, NBC recommends 65 %. On the Delhi cap the legal footprint is about
1,230 sq ft — roughly 120 sq ft *less* per floor than drawn.

**Status: concept design / spatial feasibility. Not a sanction drawing.**
