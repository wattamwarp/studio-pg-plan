# Design Ideas — sourced, and what each one is worth here

Not rules. Ideas from built projects and current practice, filtered for one question:
**does it work on a deep plot where only the garden and the road can breathe?**

---

## The three that actually change this building

### 1. Make the staircase the light shaft

> *"If you cannot spare floor area for a courtyard — common on a 20-foot frontage — the
> staircase becomes your best friend. Run the stair as an open vertical void from ground to
> roof and cap it with an operable skylight. You have just built a light shaft and a
> ventilation chimney in the space you were going to lose to the stair anyway."*
> — [Studio Matrx, narrow plot strategies](https://www.studiomatrx.org/guides/narrow-plot-design-strategies-india)

**Why it matters here.** Our stair already has to sit in the blind north-east corner — the
deadest point on the site, where two windowless walls meet. An open-well dog-leg with a
skylight over it turns that dead corner into the brightest thing on the floor and lights the
far end of the corridor for **zero extra area**. This is the single best idea found.

**What it needs:** an open well between the flights instead of a solid spine wall, a glazed
mumty, and light-coloured or mirrored walls in the shaft.

---

### 2. The slot void — "the building's lungs"

> AVIRA Co-Living, Makassar: a 4.8 m wide shophouse, 30 m deep, dark and damp. MIV Architects
> cut *"a continuous void, ranging from **30 cm to 50 cm** in width, from the second floor up
> to the roof… This surgical incision acts as the building's lungs, generating a stack effect
> that draws hot air out through the skylight while allowing natural light to wash over the
> deepest parts of the corridor. The once-dark hallway is thus transformed into a luminous
> circulation space."*
> — [AmazingArchitecture](https://www.amazingarchitecture.com/commercial-architecture/avira-co-living-makassar-city-indonesia-by-miv-architects)

**Why it matters here.** A full 10 × 10 ft light court costs ~100 sq ft per floor — a whole
room. A **1 ft × 40 ft slot** along the blind east wall costs **40 sq ft** and does most of the
work: it lights the corridor for its whole length, gives every bathroom on that side a real
opening instead of a duct, and drives stack ventilation up and out through a roof skylight.

**The move:** run the slot tight against the Sikandar party wall, from first floor to roof,
skylit. It never touches the neighbour and it converts our worst wall into our air supply.

---

### 3. The L-shaped micro-suite

> *"In a 10 m² twin room, an L-shaped micro-suite layout created a natural flow — beds along
> one wall, storage wrapping into the short leg, and a compact desk tucked near the window.
> Guests felt less boxed-in because **the L broke sightlines and defined zones without extra
> walls**… Aim for a 900–1000 mm clear route where possible; it feels generous."*
> — [Coohom, hostel small-room design](https://www.coohom.com/in/article/5-design-ideas-for-hostel-small-rooms)

**Why it matters here.** This is exactly your two requirements — beds split, and clear floor
to walk into on opening the door — solved by geometry rather than by adding area. Put the two
beds perpendicular to each other in an L, and the void inside the L becomes the entry landing.
No bed is ever in the doorway, and the beds don't face each other.

Their 900–1000 mm clear route matches the 3′-0″ foot-of-bed figure independently.

## Worth stealing

### 4. Alcove beds with a curtain, not a partition

The Kanso hostel in Madrid ([Stone Designs](https://www.designboom.com/design/kanso-hostel-interior-plan-stone-designs-madrid-12-13-2022/)) sets its beds
into **oak-panelled cubicles** rather than leaving them loose in the room. Coohom makes the
same point: *"curtain rails and headboard panels add acoustic softness and reduce light spill
— a small detail that supports sleep quality."*

For a twin PG this is cheap and high-impact. Line each bed with a shallow headboard panel
carrying a reading light, socket and a shelf, and hang a curtain track. Each occupant gets a
private zone worth far more than the 6 inches it costs.

### 5. Storage in the section, not the plan

Every source converges here: *"wall-mounted desks, foldable furniture, and storage shelves
above or beneath beds maintain floor clarity."* Beds on 400–600 mm legs give a full luggage
drawer underneath — which for a PG is the difference between a tidy room and a corridor full
of suitcases.

### 6. Push the corridor below 10 % of the floor

> *"In conventional apartment buildings, circulation consumes 15 to 20 percent of the total
> floor area. On a narrow site, that figure must drop **below 10 percent** to keep units large
> enough for comfortable living."*
> — [VOID+, Tehran, via build-construct](https://build-construct.com/architecture/void-strategies-for-multi-unit-residential-buildings-on-narrow-urban-sites/)

Ours is currently 138 sq ft of 1,343 — **10.3 %**. Right at the line, which says the 3′-0″
corridor is the correct call and the length is what needs attacking, not the width.

### 7. Borrowed light across the depth

VOID+ runs rooms up to 12 m deep and makes them work by giving each unit **a second light
source on the opposite side**, plus sliding partitions that open for cross-ventilation in the
afternoon and close for privacy at night. Applied here: a high-level internal window from each
bedroom into the skylit slot gives every room cross-ventilation, which is otherwise impossible
on a single-aspect plan.

### 8. Split-level, if you ever go taller

Both [Co-Living Home, Hsinchu](https://globaldesignnews.com/wedged-within-a-narrow-plot-this-co-living-apartment-by-yuan-architects-rethinks-the-flow-of-traditional-residential-apartments-for-new-possibilities-of-living-together-in-the-city/)
(4 m wide base) and AVIRA use **interlocking split sections** — sleeping deck above, living and
washing below — to make a narrow unit feel generous. Needs 4.5 m floor-to-floor, so it is a
different building, but worth knowing it exists.

## Considered and rejected

| Idea | Why not here |
| --- | --- |
| **Bunk beds / privacy pods** | Would let a 100 m² floor take far more beds — but you asked for two separate beds, and bunks are the one thing that contradicts it. Noted only so the cost of that decision is visible: bunking would roughly double capacity. |
| **Spiral stair** (4–6 ft dia., 30–40 % less floor than a straight run) | Tempting, but a PG stair carries suitcases and is the only escape route. [Codes typically restrict alternating-tread and spiral stairs to secondary access](https://highcountrymetalworks.com/post/staircase-ideas). Keep the dog-leg; make it a light shaft instead. |
| **Alternating-tread "ship's stair"** | Cuts the run 30–50 %, but restricted to fewer than 5 occupants / secondary egress. Not for a primary hostel stair. |
| **External / alleyway stair** | Preserves interior area and is lovely on the garden side — but it would eat the one facade that has to carry bedroom windows. |
| **Full 10 × 10 ft light court** | Costs a whole room per floor. The 1 ft slot buys most of the benefit for a third of the area. |

## The scheme these ideas add up to

```
        GARDEN — slanted, the only good long edge
              ↓
   ┌───────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  BEDROOM  BEDROOM  BEDROOM  BEDROOM   │   all bedrooms on the
   │   ┌──┐     ┌──┐     ┌──┐     ┌──┐     │   garden + road faces
   │   │L │     │L │     │L │     │L │     │   L = L-shaped bed pair,
   │   └──┘     └──┘     └──┘     └──┘     │   entry void inside the L
   ├───────────────────────────────────────┤
   │  bath  bath  bath  bath   ← inboard   │   baths in the dim zone,
   ├───────────────────────────────────────┤   opening onto the slot
   │ ░░░░░ CORRIDOR — lit by the slot ░░░░ │
   ├─────┬─────────────────────────────────┤
   │ ▓▓▓ │  1 ft SLOT VOID, skylit ────────│ ← the "lungs", against
   │STAIR│  stack ventilation up and out   │   the blind party wall
   │skylit                                 │
   └─────┴─────────────────────────────────┘
              ↑
        SIKANDAR — blind party wall
```

Four moves, in order of value:

1. **Bedrooms take the whole garden and road frontage.** Nothing else goes on a good wall.
2. **Bathrooms sit inboard**, in the zone that was going to be dark anyway, opening onto the
   slot rather than onto a duct.
3. **A 1 ft skylit slot** runs the length of the blind east wall — lungs and light for the
   corridor and the bathrooms, at 40 sq ft a floor.
4. **The stair is an open well with a skylight**, sitting in the deadest corner and lighting
   the far end of the corridor for free.

Inside each room: two beds in an **L**, alcove-lined with a curtain and a headboard panel,
drawers underneath, a wardrobe wrapping the short leg, and the entry void inside the L.
